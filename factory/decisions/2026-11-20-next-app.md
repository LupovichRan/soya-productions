# Decision: which app is next

Made 2026-11-20 by the decision maker (standard method), model Phi-3.5 mini Instruct 4-bit on llama.cpp, four CPU cores, 299 seconds. Reversible, medium stakes.

**Question:** Which concept should Soyactory build as its next small web app?

**Chosen: Say It Kindly.** Confidence 0.5 (medium). A close call: 1 point.

| Option | Total | Understands why | Current idea used well | One session | Different from existing apps | Stays on device |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Say It Kindly** | 90.5 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 5 | 5 |
| Second Look | 89.5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 |
| Plain Steps | 72.6 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 5 |

Weights the model set before it saw the options: understands why 5, current idea used well 4, one session 3, different from existing apps 2, stays on device 5.

**What the model said against its own choice (pre-mortem):** the rewriting may be too complex to do well; people may hesitate to paste a personal message even when told it stays on the device. Early warning for each: testers overwhelmed by the variety of answers; testers hesitating at the paste box.

**Cautions it raised:** the 1-point margin; it doubted the leader and said the criteria miss who the app is for; it suggested an option not on the list (an on-device assistant for daily tasks).

**Read this critically:**
- The 1-point gap is well inside what one different answer could change. "Second Look" scored higher on buildability (4 against 2), and "Say It Kindly" scored 2 there because rewriting a message well is the hardest of the three to do. That risk is real, not noise.
- The backlog's own scores also put Say It Kindly first (24 against 21), so two independent ways of choosing agree.

## Second and third runs (same question, same context)

| Run | Model and method | Say It Kindly | Second Look | Plain Steps | Margin | Confidence | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phi-3.5 mini, standard | 90.5 | 89.5 | 72.6 | 1.0 | 0.5 | 299 s |
| 2 | Phi-3.5 mini, standard | 96.8 | 70.5 | 66.3 | 26.3 | 0.5 | 270 s |
| 3 | **Qwen2.5 7B, deep** (each criterion asked three times, option order rotated) | 84.2 | 80.0 | 75.8 | 4.2 | 0.3 | 745 s |

**All three runs choose Say It Kindly, and in all three Second Look is second and Plain Steps last.** The choice is stable. The margin is not: between runs 1 and 2 the same model scored Second Look 89.5 and then 70.5. A margin of a few points is noise; the order of the three is the signal.

The deep run on the stronger model is the least sure of itself (confidence 0.3), and it flagged: the scores for one option varied a lot between its three rounds; one reason it gave ("addresses a common social anxiety issue") is an assumption that needs real users to confirm; and a doubt that the criteria miss "user engagement and adoption". Its pre-mortem names a risk worth taking seriously: that Say It Kindly looks too similar to Roundtable in how it works (a text box in, a reading out).

**What this changes:** nothing about the choice. It adds one instruction to the build: make Say It Kindly feel different from the existing apps, and keep it to one box and one button.

**Decision record:** decided 2026-11-20, confirmed by three runs on two models. Review 2026-11-27: was it built, did the simple (non-AI) version help, and was the rewriting good enough to ship? What would change the choice: evidence that people will not paste personal messages into any app.

**Next step (the model's, edited):** build Say It Kindly with a rule-based simple version that works without AI, in English and Hebrew, and the browser's built-in model as an optional extra, labelled "Written by AI".
