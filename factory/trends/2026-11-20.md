# Trend brief — 2026-11-20

The first brief, written while setting the factory up. The searches returned little dated to this month; most sources are from late 2025 to mid 2026. Treat it as a starting map, not as news.

## In one paragraph

Software is moving from AI that answers to AI that acts, and the people building it are organising that work into "software factories" where agents build and people set intent and review. Two things matter most for small apps made for ordinary people: a model now ships inside the desktop Chrome browser, so a web page can use AI with nothing leaving the device and no key; and the gap in consumer AI is not capability but simplicity — tools that need no prompt-writing and no set-up.

## What has shipped

- **A model inside the browser.** Chrome's Prompt API lets a web page ask the browser's own on-device model. Reported as stable for web pages from Chrome 148 (May 2026). Desktop only; Android was still being prototyped. A page can check `LanguageModel.availability()` first, so it works as an optional extra. Two browser vendors oppose the specification, so it is not a cross-browser baseline.
- **Bring-your-own model in the browser.** WebGPU with libraries such as Transformers.js runs a downloaded model in the page. Stable in Chrome and Edge; partial elsewhere. Heavier than the built-in route.
- **Agents that write most of the code.** Spotify reported more than 1,500 merged agent-written pull requests (November 2025). A public two-week experiment had agents build, test, review and deploy a note-taking app. Warp announced "Factories" for agent loops (August 2026).

## What is forecast or claimed

- Gartner is cited as projecting that about 40% of enterprise apps will include task-specific agents by the end of 2026 — and, separately, that over 40% of agent projects may be cancelled by 2027, with a warning about overstated "agent" marketing.
- Consultancies describe factories run by as few as three engineers. This is self-reported; one overview notes there is no benchmark for any of it yet.
- Consumer round-ups list personal finance and health coaching, voice tutoring and plain-language automations as the strongest directions. Most of these lists are unvalidated ideas from vendors and blogs.

## What this means for small apps

1. **On-device AI fits the Soya rules exactly.** No key, no server, nothing leaves the phone or computer. It must be an optional extra with a labelled sample path, because most phones do not have it yet.
2. **Remove the prompt.** The useful consumer pattern is a form with two or three plain boxes, not a chat box.
3. **Review is the bottleneck.** Every factory source ends in the same place: people define intent and review outcomes. This factory keeps one film per run and stops building when reviews pile up.
4. **Be wary of the word "agent".** A film should do one visible, checkable thing.

## Concepts worth a film

See `factory/backlog.md` for scores.

- **Say It Kindly** — paste a message you are nervous to send; get a calmer, clearer version. Uses the browser's own model when present, with a labelled sample otherwise.
- **Plain Steps** — describe a chore you repeat; get it back as a short numbered routine you can tick off.
- **Read It To Me Simply** — paste a confusing letter or notice; get what it says, what it asks of you, and by when. Not advice.
- **Second Look** — paste a product's AI claim; get plain questions to ask before believing it.
- **Five-Minute Teacher** — pick a topic; get one small spoken lesson and one question back.

## The decision

Which concept to build was put to the decision maker (standard method, reversible, medium stakes), choosing between the three highest-scoring concepts. The model was Qwen2.5 7B Instruct (4-bit), run on llama.cpp inside the build session. It had not yet been through calibration when it was asked; see the decision maker's README for how it did afterwards.

| Option | Total | Understands why | Tries on-device AI | One session | Differs from Roundtable | Nothing leaves device |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Read It To Me Simply** | 85.3 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| Say It Kindly | 81.3 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| Plain Steps | 68.0 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 5 | 5 |

Weights it set before seeing the options: on-device AI 5, nothing leaves the device 4, understands why 3, differs from Roundtable 2, one session 1.

- **Chosen:** Read It To Me Simply. Confidence: medium. Margin: 4 points, a close call.
- **Pre-mortem:** it may fail to show what on-device AI can really do, since it only simplifies text. Early warning: the reading with AI is not noticeably better than the reading without.
- **Bias it named:** confirmation.
- **One of its reasons was wrong.** It scored Say It Kindly 2 on "nothing leaves the device" because "pasting a message involves data leaving the device". That is false: both concepts keep the text on the device. Without that error Say It Kindly would have scored about 93 and won. The stress-test step has since been changed to look for reasons that contradict the context.
- **What was done:** the playbook says to build the decision maker's choice unless it breaks a rule. It breaks none, so Read It To Me Simply was built, as "Read It Simply". Say It Kindly stays at the top of the backlog. The backlog's own scores had it one point ahead (24 to 23), so either order was defensible.

Decision record: decided 2026-11-20; review on 2026-11-27 — was the film merged, and is the reading with the browser's AI clearly better than the simple reading?
