# Concept: an open-source home for ideas, built by agents

Source: Ran's notes of 2026-11-20 (Hebrew, paraphrased) and the preview video he shared.

## In one paragraph
Like GitHub, but for ideas. Anyone can bring an idea, test it fast, and gather a community around it. AI agents behind the scenes do the building. The more a person takes part (backing, doing work, answering surveys), the more voice and the more of a say they get in the idea. It is a head start, but you get a voice, influence, belonging and involvement. The goal is to build an audience for an idea or product, and to find out quickly whether it deserves to exist. Soycatory is the open-source software factory behind it.

## What the preview video shows (four features)
1. **Discover**: pick a project you like, back it for a small amount or help with a task, and your say grows with what you put in.
2. **The project room**: a mascot fronts each idea, with teaser, panel, channel, roadmap, tasks, ledger and verdict tabs.
3. **A voice**: coalitions, surveys and a shared roadmap. Backing is a seat; accepted work is a louder voice. A voice cannot be bought beyond the backing.
4. **Rehearsal, then verdict**: a two-week test with real signals (sign-ups, referrals, pledges, calls). Real people decide: continue, change direction, or stop.

## How it fits what we already have
- Roundtable is the single-person step: a panel of simulated reviewers, labelled as a sample. This concept is the step after: real people.
- The factory already makes the small apps. Agents would build what the community's roadmap accepts, and each result is still a pull request that a person reviews.
- "Do not invent demand" stays: signals must be real people, never simulated.

## Open questions for Ran (not decided)
1. **Percentages in an idea.** "More involvement, more percent" is ownership. That is a legal and tax matter (in Israel too). Safe first step: involvement earns voice and credit in a public ledger, no ownership. Real equity only through a lawyer-drafted agreement.
2. **Open-source licence.** Needs a choice only Ran can make (for example MIT or Apache-2.0). Nothing is added to the repository until he picks one.
3. **Accounts and a server.** A community needs sign-in and shared storage, which the current rules rule out (static, localStorage only). Decide whether the first version is a static, read-only showcase with sign-ups through a form, or a new app with a backend.
4. **Money.** The video shows one-dollar backing. Taking real payments adds rules (payments, refunds, tax). Start with free backing: a pledge of time, not money.

## Suggested first small app
"Idea page": one public page per idea with the teaser, a two-week signal count (real clicks on "I want this", "I will tell a friend", "I will help"), and a plain verdict at the end. Static, no accounts, one form for sign-ups.
